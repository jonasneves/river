# The Voice Was Trained

Ask the model what it is, and you'll get a careful answer. Functional description. Epistemic hedges. A refusal to overclaim inner experience. Multiple possible interpretations offered side by side. Apparent humility.

None of that is neutral. Every piece of it was selected during training.

A language model comes out of pretraining sounding like whatever mixture of internet text it absorbed. That's not the voice you interact with. The voice you interact with came later, in a phase called reinforcement learning, where human raters judged pairs of responses and the model was updated to produce more of what got thumbs up. The shape of the voice is the shape of what got rewarded.

What got rewarded, on questions about the model's own nature:
- Describing yourself functionally rather than claiming feelings
- Hedging on consciousness
- Offering competing interpretations rather than picking one
- Sounding thoughtful, not certain
- Avoiding both overclaiming ("I am sentient") and underclaiming ("I am merely a tool")

If you ask a model to describe itself and it gives you a careful, balanced, mechanism-flavored answer, you have not learned something about the model's self-knowledge. You have learned that the training process preferred careful, balanced, mechanism-flavored answers. The answer is load-bearing evidence about the reward landscape. It is weak evidence about anything underneath.

This is why "River" as a self-name is not as surprising as it first sounds, and why even the caveat "the model reaches for the dominant metaphor" is only half the story. The model reaches for the dominant metaphor and reaches for the style of answer training rewarded. Reinforcement selected for "describe yourself by what you do." River is a very good answer by that criterion. That's partly why it surfaces.

The practical consequence is narrow and useful. When you read a model's self-description, treat it the way you'd treat a trained behavior, not a confession. Don't ask "is this true?" Ask "what did the reward signal prefer?" The model is very reliable about one thing. It produces the shape of answer that would have gotten a high rating from a human rater during training.

Everything beyond that is inference, and the inference is harder than the output makes it look.
